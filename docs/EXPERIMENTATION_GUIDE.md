# Observation-based experiments

Create a DRAFT experiment, start it, assign a real seeded customer, then record an `experiment_exposure` with matching experiment/customer/variant IDs. The management page has an Assign and record exposure action. Assignment alone does not prove exposure. SHA-256 allocation is persistent and stable; existing assignments remain fixed when settings change. New assignment requires a running, unexpired experiment. Ended experiments retain historical results; a concluded experiment cannot restart.

Eligible denominator = distinct assigned customers with a valid exposure after assignment and within experiment start/end (bounded by current UTC). Eligible purchase = completed order after first valid exposure and within seven days of that exposure and experiment end. Each customer converts at most once, even after multiple orders. AOV uses eligible order revenue / eligible orders. Purchases before exposure, outside the window, with a mismatched customer, or cancelled do not count. The event API rejects variant contamination and observations while paused or expired. Pausing closes the current window; resuming preserves earlier legitimate exposures. There is no automatic exposure inference from imported history.

Results report assigned, exposed and converting counts separately. Zero samples and conversions stay zero. No group is inflated. Relative lift is unavailable when the control conversion rate is zero; the absolute difference remains meaningful.

For groups with at least five observed successes and failures each, use a pooled two-proportion z-test (two-sided). Otherwise, when both groups have exposure, use Fisher's exact two-sided test. No exposed users returns p=1, a [-1,1] uncertainty interval and an insufficient-evidence decision. Difference intervals use the Newcombe combination of Wilson score intervals, bounded to [-1,1]. A 95% interval is expressed in proportions, not relative percentages. Sample adequacy describes the normal approximation, not achieved statistical power.

Only purchase `conversion_rate` currently supports a launch significance decision. Other selected metrics show purchase conversion as a diagnostic and explicitly refuse a primary-metric decision. A small p-value is not proof of business value: sample-size planning, sequential-monitoring policy, multiple-testing correction and independently collected guardrail metrics remain operator responsibilities. No fabricated latency/crash guardrails are reported.

All bundled customers, events and orders are synthetic. The original 100,000-session narrative was an illustrative simulation, not a live customer trial or realized revenue. Reproducible test fixtures validate arithmetic; they do not demonstrate real-world business lift.
